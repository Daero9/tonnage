Privacy policy

Tonnup app — last updated: 26 October 2026
Translated from French for convenience. If the two versions differ, the French version prevails.

In one sentence: Tonnup collects no personal data. Everything you enter stays in your phone's memory. No server receives your workouts, your weight or your meals.

Four things can leave the device, each only if you ask for it: the barcode you scan, sent to a food database; the photo of a plate, sent to Anthropic for analysis; if you turn on backup, a copy of your data stored in your own Google Drive; and, if you turn on My friends, your nickname and a few consistency figures, which you choose to show or not. Everything is detailed below.

Who is responsible

The data controller is Nicolas Poncin, a sole trader operating under the name Inncop Project, 229 rue Saint-Honoré, 75001 Paris, France — SIREN 831 467 907. Contact: inncopproject@gmail.com.

In practice, he has access to none of your data: it stays on your phone, or in your own Google Drive if you asked for it — a space he cannot access either.

What data the app handles

Tonnup records, only on your device: your first name, sex, age, height, weight and weight goal; your workouts, exercises, sets, loads and reps; your weigh-ins; your cardio sessions; the foods you tick; your display settings; and, if you pick one, your profile photo and your companion's accessories.

The profile photo is optional. It is shrunk to a small thumbnail (256 pixels) and saved on your phone only; it is never sent to anyone. If you turn on Google Drive backup, it goes into your backup file, on your own Drive, like everything else.

This information is used to calculate your calorie needs, your training loads and your progress charts. It is used for nothing else.

Where it is stored

In your browser's local storage (localStorage and IndexedDB), on your phone. By default, it is not transmitted, not backed up remotely and not synced between devices.

Important consequence: if you uninstall the app, clear your browser data or change phones, this information disappears — unless you turned on the Google Drive backup described below, or saved a backup file from Settings → Backup details.

Google Drive backup (optional)

From the settings, you can ask Tonnup to keep a copy of your data in your own Google Drive. Nothing is turned on without your action: you have to sign in with your Google account and accept the requested permission.

  • What is sent: a single file containing all your Tonnup data (profile, workouts, weigh-ins, meals, settings). It is updated a few seconds after each change.
  • Where it goes: into the app-reserved space of your Drive (“appDataFolder”). This space is invisible in your usual Drive, counts only for you, and the publisher cannot access it: only the app, from your phone and with your permission, can read and write there.
  • What the app asks Google for: only the drive.appdata permission, limited to that space. It sees neither your other files, nor your contacts, nor your emails, and does not read your email address. The use of data obtained through Google APIs complies with the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.
  • To stop: turn off backup in the settings; you can also remove Tonnup's access from your Google account (Security → Your connections to third-party apps & services), which deletes the file. This file is also covered by Google's privacy policy.

Barcode scanning

From the Nutrition screen, you can scan a product's barcode to fill in its calories and protein automatically. It is the only feature of the app that uses the network and the camera. It only starts if you press the scan button.

What is sent: only the digits of the barcode, for example 3033490004743. Nothing else — not your name, your weight or what you eat, nor any device or account identifier.

To whom: to Open Food Facts, an open, collaborative food database published by the Open Food Facts association (France), a non-profit organisation. Its privacy policy is available on its website.

What comes back: the product's name, brand, nutritional values and the address of its photo. This information is then saved on your phone, like the rest — except for the photo, see below.

The product photo: if Open Food Facts has one, it is downloaded from images.openfoodfacts.org to be shown on the product card. This second request, too, sends nothing but the image address. The photo is neither saved in your data nor included in your backups: it goes through the browser's temporary memory, which your phone empties on its own. If no photo exists, or if you are offline, a simple icon replaces it.

If you never scan: no request is made, and nothing leaves the device.

Photo of a plate (optional)

From the “Scan my meal” screen, you can photograph your plate to get an estimate of what it contains. This feature only runs if you press the shutter: nothing is sent without your action.

  • What is sent: the photo, shrunk (1,000 pixels at most), and, if you wrote one, your note (“it's fish”). Not your name, your weight or your history — the photo travels alone.
  • Which way it goes: through a relay run by the publisher (hosted by Cloudflare), which only passes it on and keeps neither the photo nor the answer. The relay receives a random device identifier, created by the app and unrelated to your identity, only to limit the number of analyses per minute. To prevent abuse, it also records, for 48 hours at most, your IP address and the number of photos requested from it (a simple counter, without the photo); this address is neither matched with your identity nor used for anything else, and the counter is kept in the European Union.
  • Who analyses it: Anthropic (Anthropic, PBC, San Francisco, USA), the maker of the model that recognises the food. Under its API terms, Anthropic does not use what it receives to train its models, and may keep it for a limited time (thirty days at most) for the security of its service, before deletion. This transfer outside the European Union is covered by the European Commission's standard contractual clauses.
  • What stays with you: the photo is neither saved by the app nor added to your gallery; only the foods you confirm are logged in your diary, as if you had entered them by hand.
  • Limit: ten photos per day and per device with Tonnup Premium (one during the free trial), plus the photo packs (20 or 50) bought in Google Play.

My friends (optional)

From Profile → My friends, you can follow your friends' consistency, and they can follow yours. This feature is off until you turn it on: you have to choose a nickname, confirm that you are 16 or older, and press “Turn on”.

  • No account to create: no email, no password. The app receives an anonymous identifier, created at random and unrelated to your identity.
  • What is sent: your nickname and its number (for example Lea#4821), your companion's accessories, colour and mood, your friend code, and — depending on your settings — your streak (activities in a row) and your best streak, your number of workouts, your week (active days, total kilos lifted, kilometres and training time) and your last workout (its name, its duration or distance, and when). Your friends list, friend requests, the people you block and any reports you make are also recorded.
  • What is never sent: your weight, height, age, meals, photos, the details of your workouts (exercises, loads, sets) and your location.
  • Who sees it — you choose, in My friends → My profile → Who sees what. For each piece of information: Public (your nickname, your companion and, if you wish, your streaks appear in search and in the “All of Tonnup” leaderboard), Friends (only the people you accepted) or Only me (the information does not leave your phone). Anything beyond your nickname, your companion and your streaks is never public.
  • Where it is stored: with Google Firebase (Google Ireland Limited), in the European Union (europe-west1 region, Belgium). Access is protected by rules that only let you and your friends read your card; the publisher can consult the database for maintenance and moderation.
  • Why: only to run the “My friends” feature and the leaderboard, which you chose to turn on. Nothing is used for advertising or sold.
  • Moderation: you can block or report someone. A report contains your anonymous identifier, that of the reported person and the reason; only the publisher reads it. An inappropriate nickname or an impersonation can be removed by the publisher.
  • How long: until you delete this data. A card left inactive for more than twelve months may be erased.
  • To erase everything: My friends → My profile → Delete my friends data. Your nickname, code, card, friendships, requests, blocks and anonymous identifier are deleted from Firebase immediately. Your workouts stay on your phone.

Smartwatch (optional)

On Android 14 or later, Tonnup can read the activities recorded by your watch (Amazfit/Zepp, Samsung Health, Fitbit, Garmin Connect…) through Health Connect, Android's health service. Nothing happens until you turn it on in Settings → Smartwatch and grant permission in Health Connect.

  • What is read: your activity sessions (type, start and end time) and, over the duration of each session, the distance, active calories and heart rate; your daily step count (and during a session). Nothing else: not your weight, nor any other health data.
  • Your VO2max, only if you choose: with “Track my VO2max”, Tonnup reads the VO2max estimated by your watch to show you how it evolves.
  • Your sleep, only if you choose: with “Take my sleep into account”, Tonnup reads the length of your nights measured by your watch (start, end, time asleep; nights entered by hand are ignored). It uses it only to make the day's workout more cautious after a very bad night. The durations stay on your phone, 30 days at most, and are erased when you turn the option off.
  • What it is for: to suggest adding these activities to Tonnup, with their real distance, calories and your heart rate — you choose for each one: add it or ignore it — and to show the heart rate, calories and distance measured by your watch on your Tonnup workouts (heart-rate curve, zones, pace).
  • Nothing is written: Tonnup only reads. It adds, changes and deletes no data in Health Connect.
  • Where it goes: nowhere. The activities you add join your Tonnup data on your phone (and in your Google Drive backup if you turned it on). They are never sent to the publisher, to Firebase, to Anthropic or to anyone, and are not used for any advertising. The use of this data complies with Google's rules on Health Connect data (limited use).
  • To stop: turn off “Suggest my activities” in Tonnup, or remove Tonnup's access in the Health Connect settings. Activities already added stay in your history, and are erased like the rest (“Erase everything”).

The camera

The camera is used to read a barcode and, if you decide to, to photograph a plate — only while the scan screen is open.

  • On Android, the camera image is analysed live on the phone. Nothing is recorded: no image is written to the device or transmitted.
  • On iPhone, no browser can read a barcode live — they all use Safari's engine. The app then decodes camera images on your phone, with a built-in reader. Nothing is added to your gallery, nothing is kept, nothing is sent. Only the digits read are then used for the search.
  • The photo of a plate, however, is sent for analysis — see the previous section. It is the only case where an image leaves the phone.

Camera permission is requested at the first scan. You can refuse it: the app then offers to type the barcode digits by hand, and everything else keeps working.

What the app does not do

  • No account to create, no email address requested (the “My friends” feature uses an anonymous identifier).
  • No advertising, no trackers, no audience measurement tools.
  • No analytics library from another publisher is included in the app.
  • None of your personal data is sold or shared for advertising. The only third parties that receive anything are those described here, at your request: Open Food Facts (a barcode), Anthropic (a photo of a plate), Google Drive (your backup, in your own account) and Google Firebase (the “My friends” feature).
  • No access to your contacts, location, microphone or files — except, if you choose it, an image from your gallery for the analysis of a plate.
  • No photos saved by the app, no video or audio recording.

Permissions requested on Android

The app only asks for what it actually uses:

  • Camera — to read a barcode or photograph a plate. Requested at the first scan, can be refused, and the app stays usable without it.
  • Notifications — to tell you when a rest period ends, with your phone put away. These notifications are created on your phone: they go through no server, and no one can send you any remotely.
  • Keep the screen awake — during a workout, if you turn the option on.
  • Vibration — for haptic feedback.
  • Google Play Billing — for the “Tonnup Premium” subscription. Payment is made with Google; the app never sees your bank details.
  • Google account — only if you turn on Drive backup, to obtain the permission described above.
  • Health Connect (read only: sessions, distance, steps, active calories, heart rate, VO2max if you track it, and sleep if you turn on “Take my sleep into account”) — only if you turn on the smartwatch, see above. Requested on activation, can be refused, and the app stays usable without it.

Two versions, two ways of working

Installed from the Play Store, the app contains all its files: it works entirely offline. It only contacts the network for the features described here, at your request: scanning (Open Food Facts), the photo of a plate (relay then Anthropic), Google Drive backup and, if you turned it on, the “My friends” feature (Google Firebase).

Opened in a browser, it also fetches its updates from its host (GitHub Pages) at launch. No personal data is sent with this request, and once loaded it also works offline.

In both cases, this page remains available online: Google requires a privacy policy that can be read even before installation.

What Google knows

If you installed Tonnup from the Play Store, Google records on its side the installation of the app and, where applicable, the purchase of a subscription. This processing is covered by Google's privacy policy, not by this page.

The publisher only receives anonymous statistics from Google — number of downloads, countries, ratings — never the identity or data of a user. If a subscription is taken out, Google is the seller: it collects the payment, handles VAT and passes the amount due to the publisher, without bank details.

Deleting your data

To request the deletion of your Tonnup data, you don't need to ask anyone: you delete it yourself, immediately.

If you turned on “My friends”: My friends → My profile → Delete my friends data erases everything stored with Firebase (see above). If you no longer have access to the app, write to the address below with your nickname and its number: deletion is done within thirty days.

From the app: Settings → Backup details → Erase everything. Deletion is immediate and permanent. You can also uninstall the app or clear the site data from your browser settings: the result is the same.

If Google Drive backup was on, “Erase everything” also deletes the copy in your Drive. You can also remove Tonnup's access from your Google account at any time (Google Account → Security → Your connections to third-party apps & services).

What is deleted: everything — profile, workouts, weigh-ins, meals, settings, and the Drive copy. What is kept: nothing. No additional retention period: there is no copy with the publisher. A photo of a plate already sent to Anthropic follows the rule described above (thirty days at most, then deletion).

For any question, write to inncopproject@gmail.com with the subject “Tonnup — data deletion”.

Minimum age

Tonnup is for people aged 16 or older. Calorie-need calculations rely on formulas established for adults, and weight tracking is not suitable for a younger audience. The app collects no data, so it never processes data about minors.

Health

Tonnup is a fitness-tracking app, not a medical device. The calories, protein and loads shown are estimates. They do not replace the advice of a doctor, a dietitian or a coach. If you have a medical condition, an injury, are pregnant or are under treatment, seek medical advice before following the app's recommendations.

Nutritional values from Open Food Facts are entered by the community and by brands: they may contain errors. Check the packaging if in doubt, especially for an allergen.

The analysis of a photo of a plate is an automatic estimate: the foods recognised, their quantities and their values are orders of magnitude, always flagged as such in the app, and can be edited before saving. It does not detect allergens, invisible cooking oils or anything outside the frame.
